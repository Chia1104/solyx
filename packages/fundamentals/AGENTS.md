# `@solyx/fundamentals`

Fundamentals providers, one module per provider, each implementing the contracts of `@solyx/core/fundamentals` it serves. `./finmind` is a `FundamentalsProvider`: it reads Taiwan listings' quarterly income statements, balance sheets and cash flows, monthly revenue and dividends, who trades them and the market (`FlowsProvider` from `@solyx/core/flows`), and the days Taiwan's exchange trades (`TradingCalendarProvider` from `@solyx/core/session`), from FinMind's open API, which answers without a token under the lowest limit and with the user's token under the limit of the plan it belongs to (`FINMIND_PLANS`). The token and the plan are read for every request, so a saved one applies at once. `./tw-exchanges` is an `ExchangeListsProvider` and a `ClosuresProvider` (from `@solyx/core/session`): it reads the restrictions the Taiwan Stock Exchange and the Taipei Exchange list for their listings, the multiples they work out for them, their notices of shares going ex, their companies' shareholders' meetings, each company with the industry they class it in and the shares it has issued, the price limits they set for each listing's next session and the funds the Taiwan Stock Exchange lists, from the exchanges' open data, which needs no key, and the weekdays the Taiwan Stock Exchange closes the market over the year, which the Taipei Exchange keeps too. The Taipei Exchange publishes no list of its funds, so a fund listed there has no profile. `./sitca` is a `FundReportsProvider`: it reads every domestic fund's newest net asset value and what each paid out of its assets over the newest month from the files the Securities Investment Trust & Consulting Association publishes as government open data, which need no key and cover the funds listed on either exchange.

## Boundaries

- Normalize here to the shapes `@solyx/core` sets: a quarter without revenue is dropped, as is a balance sheet without total assets, a cash flow line a provider gives from the year's start is taken down to the quarter alone against the quarter before, and left empty where that one does not report it, flows count shares, not board lots, and the rows a provider splits one investor group into add up to one. A provider that gives no filing date stamps `knownFrom` with the market's filing deadline, so a past multiple never leans on results the market had not seen. A distribution that pays neither cash nor stock, such as a cash capital increase alone, is dropped, and one no announcement carries, as a fund's, keeps `announced` empty and takes its place from the provider's own date for it.
- A row that does not read is dropped, but an answer none of whose rows read fails: that is the provider writing the dataset another way, never a listing with nothing filed.
- Each module keeps within its provider's request limit with `@solyx/utils/rate-limit`, so callers keep one instance.
- A row of another listing is dropped, since a FinMind dataset may answer for every listing at once.
- An exchange's list is its own of today for the whole market: `./tw-exchanges` asks for each once per read and leaves how often to its caller, since the exchanges publish no limit, and reads nothing the lists no longer hold. A restriction keeps the exchange's own words as its note, and ends the day before the one an exchange names for lifting it. An attention notice, a run of them that may lead to disposition, and altered trading name no end, so each stands while the exchange's list holds it; TWSE's list of altered trading names no day, so it is dated today. A list that answers one row with no code has nothing on it. A fund needs only its code, name and kind to read; any other field the exchange leaves out, or fills with a word for nothing, is empty. The Taiwan Stock Exchange's list of funds keeps those it has delisted, so a fund missing from its list of the last session's trading, which holds the listings that did not trade too, is left out, and a read that finds that list empty fails. A multiple an exchange works none out for is empty too, and one written any other way drops its row. A notice of shares going ex carries its cash a share only where they go ex cash and the amount is announced, and says nothing of how much stock or at what price new shares are subscribed. The exchange's days of the year also name weekends and the first and last trading days around a long holiday, which are no closures and are left out. The lists of price limits hold tens of thousands of warrants, which no order names and are left out by their codes, and give a listing that trades without limits a limit-up of 9,999.95, which reads as no band. TWSE's names no session of its own, only the last day each listing traded, so its limits follow the latest of those days.
- A company's count of shares is the shares its listing trades as, so a depositary receipt's, which the lists give as its foreign issuer's shares, reads as none.
- An exchange's list classes a company by a code alone, so `./tw-exchanges` holds what the exchanges call each code, as their own lists pair the two; both exchanges class by the same codes, and one they add later reads by its code until it is named there.
- SITCA's files hold every domestic fund, listed or not, a row per share class, so `./sitca` reads each whole once per call and leaves how often to its caller. A fund is a listing's where its units carry an exchange code, which starts with a digit; a value struck in another currency than the market's is left out, since it is no unit's price there. The costs name a fund by its tax id alone, without the letter a share class adds, so they reach a listing through the values' rows, and a fund new since the month has none. A row with more or fewer fields than the file's header names is dropped. SITCA refuses a request that accepts only text, so the files are read without ky's `.text()`.
