# `@solyx/core`

The trading domain every other package builds on: market rules (tick sizes, board and odd lots, trading sessions), candles and technical indicators, the `BrokerAdapter`, `MarketDataProvider`, `MarketDataStream`, `TotalReturnProvider`, `NewsSource`, `FundamentalsProvider`, `ExchangeListsProvider`, `FundReportsProvider`, `FlowsProvider`, `TradingCalendarProvider`, `ClosuresProvider`, `MacroCalendarProvider`, `MacroFiguresProvider`, `ExchangeRateProvider`, `YieldCurveProvider`, `SentimentScorer`, `Embedder`, `WebSearch`, `WebReader`, `MemoryStore` and `ResearchStore` contracts, the `checkOrder` risk checks, the `OrderDesk` order flow and the `ResearchDesk`, which keeps reports and scored forecasts.

## Boundaries

- Pure and cross-runtime: no I/O and no Node or DOM APIs, so every process imports it. Implementations of its contracts live in their own packages.
- A listing is its market and its exchange's code and nothing more. `./rules/tw` tells the tick a Taiwan listing takes from its code, since the exchanges number funds, notes and real estate trusts apart from stocks, so no order says what kind of listing it is for.
- Technical indicators are pure functions, one value per bar and `null` while warming up, so the charts and the agent read the same numbers.
- `./levels` derives the prices a chart marks from its bars, daily and intraday. They are pure functions too, so the chart and the agent cite the same levels.
- `./session` counts regular sessions on the days a market trades, which the caller hands it: the days its exchange set, among them those its regular session closes early, and outside their span every weekday in full, the rule for a market whose holidays are not known. A US day that closes early ends its regular session at 13:00 and its after-hours at 17:00 in New York. `getSession` reads the same days, so a market is closed all day on one it does not trade, and the `OrderDesk` checks an order's session on the days its host hands it; neither knows a closure called on the day, as for a typhoon. A `MarketClosure` is a weekday an exchange has set its market closed with what it calls the day, which only names the day: whether a day trades is the trading days' to say.
- `./quote` reduces a listing's intraday bars to its newest session against the last close before it, so the symbols pane and the overview's heat map read the same numbers, and the move from that close is a pure function here too, which the agent's quotes cite.
- `./risk` holds the checks every order passes before it is proposed and again before it is sent. While the user has locked orders, every one is refused, and once a currency's sales have lost the user's limit over a market's day, or while the broker has yet to reckon one of them or cannot say what the account can spend in it, buys in it are refused while sales, which only take risk off, still go. A sale draws only on the shares the account holds that its working sales do not hold back already, as the broker reports them. The `OrderDesk` reads that day's loss from the broker's fills, each sale's gain over the average cost of what it sold, and only for a buy that has a limit. It confirms one proposal at a time, each checked once the one before has had the broker's answer, so the cash, shares and fills a check reads are what the orders before it left. A proposal keeps its premise, the session the order went to, the listing's last price and the band then, and `premiseViolations` refuses its confirmation once that session has passed or the price has moved past `MAX_PREMISE_MOVE`; a price that cannot be read is not compared, and a proposal that kept no premise is refused. A listing's price limits are its exchange's, set after a session for the next one the market trades, and `sessionBand` holds them for that session alone, so between its close and the next limits coming out an order's band is unknown. A market order is sized at its band's high, the most it could fill at, so one is refused where the listing trades without limits or its band is unknown; a limit order with no band known goes unchecked against one.
- `./backtest` runs a long-only strategy of declared rules over a listing's bars: each rule is read at a bar's close from that bar and the ones before it and acted on at the next bar's open, so no rule can see a later price, with the app's indicators, its market's costs (a Taiwan broker's commission and tax, or the SEC's fee on US sales and the tax the US withholds from a Taiwan resident's dividends) and a tick of slippage, a bar locked at its limit filling nothing, and its record measured before and after a split day apart, each stretch beside what holding the listing through it would have returned and the deepest fall it would have taken. Its indicators read prices adjusted from the bars' references, so a split or what went ex reads as no move, each bar's carried to its own terms so a later one changes none, and a rule against a price reads it as traded; orders fill at prices as traded, and a holding carries over a split or what went ex at the reference, which is what those prices assume, less the tax its costs withhold from the cash, taken from no more cash than the reference made up for. Stops and buying and holding read prices carried over as a holding is, so holding pays that tax too, and `backtestBars` reads the listing's distributions only for a run that withholds it. Rules are data rather than code, so an agent's script asks for a backtest and the engine, not the script, decides every fill, and a strategy refuses a key it does not know, so a misspelt stop fails rather than going unread. A rule may also read a line a strategy's own code worked out (`line`), handed in beside the bars, one value a bar: the code works on `strategyBars`, the run's bars adjusted on the first bar's terms, so no later bar changes an earlier one, and a rule compares such a line only with another of the strategy's lines or a value, since a bar's figures and the indicators are on other terms. Those lines count apart from the engine's series, at most `MAX_LINE_VALUES` values a run between them, and each strategy of a scan reads its own. The rules' lines, the fill prices, the limit locks and the sessions are worked out here as columns, and the `BacktestKernel` the caller hands it runs the bars, so the kernel holds no rule of a market. On bars shorter than a day a session's first bar fills after the gap from the close before, the sessions' references come from the daily bars the caller hands it, applied where one went ex or split so the bars adjust whatever their last close was, and a bar is locked when it traded at one price, its session's limit, which `twPriceLimits` in `./rules/tw` works out from the reference. A trade is timed by its bars and the equity is the account at each session's close, so a result grows with the sessions rather than the bars. Each stretch has a Sharpe ratio of the account's session returns, and a backtest's stretches a p-value of their timing against `BACKTEST_PERMUTATIONS` orderings of their bars. `scanBacktests` runs strategies each on its own over one set of lines and picks a grid's plateau, the cell whose neighbourhood had the highest mean Sharpe ratio, in-sample where there is a split; `walkForward` picks on each window's training the same way, runs each pick on the sessions after, and runs those tests as one with the holding carried, so its record is of picking on the past alone. `rollingBacktest` runs one strategy over windows of the same calendar months, one from the first session of every step of months and only whole ones, each from holding nothing at the close before it with its rules read from there, so how much a record owes to the day it started shows; it sums them up beside holding through each and counts how many fit end to end, since windows that overlap share their bars, and tests no window's timing. A result, a walk forward's too, keeps the account's value and holding's at each session's close, so a window draws one against the other. `backtestBars` reads a listing's bars for one, the app's own history or from a day on and none before it, with the daily bars that shorter ones take their sessions' references from, so the agent's backtests and a window's read alike.
- `./strategy` is a strategy the user writes: a folder whose `STRATEGY.md` frontmatter (`strategyManifestSchema`) names the command its code runs by, the numbers the code takes, bounded and stepped as a template's are, and the rules the app trades on, which read the lines the code works out. The code is handed `strategyInput`, the listing's `strategyBars` as columns and the jobs, each a set of numbers over the first so many bars, and writes each job's lines, which `readStrategyOutput` reads, a NaN or an infinity as no value; it never fills, prices or costs a trade, which stay the engine's. `strategyJobs` runs the first set again over the first fifths of the bars, and `lookAhead` names the first bar where a line differs from the whole run's, since a line read from its bar and the ones before it alone never does; that is a warning to whoever reads the result, not a refusal, since the user owns the code. A change to a strategy's files names each by a path `strategyPathSchema` takes, from the strategy's folder and never out of it, nor into what tools keep beside the code (`STRATEGY_LEFT_OUT`) in any case, since that is no part of a version.
- `./strategy-templates` sets a backtest up without writing rules: each template is a rule traders name by a few numbers, each number with its bounds and steps, and the years of daily bars that hold about twenty of its trades each side of a split. A setup refuses a number its template does not take, as a strategy refuses a key it does not know. A grid sets one number down and another across, row by row as a scan reads them, and `gridProblem` says as data why one would not run, counting each axis before making its values, such as its strategies reading more series than a scan takes, so a window can say so before it asks.
- `./review` replays the broker's fills into round trips, a listing's position from the buy that opened it to the sale that left none, each under the stance of the report its first buy's proposal kept, and weighs them as a record, wins against losses and the average win against the average loss, so the overview and the agent's `get_account` read the same figures. What the account held before the broker's fills begin is worked out from what it holds now less what they bought and sold, and a position held then opens no trip until it is sold off, since its first buy is not among them.
- `./broker` is a broker as the `OrderDesk` uses it: the account, its fills and its orders, placing one and asking it to cancel one. Every order is placed with its proposal's tag (`orderTag`), six letters and digits that every broker's own field takes, so where sending went unanswered or an exit cut it off, `reconcile` finds the order again in the broker's orders and keeps the proposal as submitted, and one the broker does not list once the session it went to is over failed for good. Reconciling only reads, and the desk tries it after each unanswered sending; its host tries it as it opens, when the user asks and whenever the broker tells of a change to the account's orders or fills (`brokerChanged`), which also has the host show the account anew. A proposal keeps the broker it was made for, by an id that names the account where the broker has several (`brokerId`): a desk opened on another dismisses it while it waits and never looks for its order. What a Taiwan account can spend is its settlement bank's balance with every trade not yet settled against it (`twSpendable` in `./rules/tw`), since trades settle on the second trading day after, today's at the most commission and tax so it errs low.
- An exit (`Exit` in `./order`) is a proposal whose order is the sale of shares held, which the broker watches the price for and sends itself (`BrokerAdapter.exits`), so it fires while the app is closed: once the listing trades at or below its stop, at the day's lower limit, and at or above its target, at that price. Its premise holds as an order's does, so one confirmed after its session or once the price has moved past `MAX_PREMISE_MOVE` is refused, and `checkExit` refuses one whose price the listing has reached already, or that sells shares the account's working sales and other exits hold back, and its last day falls from tomorrow to `MAX_EXIT_DAYS` on. Confirming it has the broker place each price as a leg, all or none; the mandate never sends one. A leg carries no tag, so the desk finds the legs of one whose placing went unanswered among those the broker watches by what they sell and when. `reconcileExits` reads what the broker still watches: once a leg of an exit is no longer watched, since it sold, expired or was taken off, the desk cancels the rest, so one sale ends the exit, and while the app is closed a second may sell only shares the account still holds.
- `./mandate` is what the user lets the desk send without asking them: the agent's limit orders on the listings and sides it names, each and each market day's under the values it sets per currency and a count of orders, to the one broker account it was set for, until it expires. `mandateViolations` says why it does not cover an order, as data. The desk puts each agent proposal that passed every check to it as it is made and keeps the verdict with the proposal; one it covers outside shadow goes the way a confirmation does, checked again with the mandate read afresh, so a mandate changed or revoked meanwhile leaves the proposal waiting for the user. A day's count is of what the mandate covered, in shadow too, so shadow shows what it would have sent, and of every order the broker lists for that day under the desk's tag, the user's own confirmed orders among them, as the broker holds it, a proposal counting only where the broker lists no order of it, since a restored backup or an edit of the store loses or changes proposals but not the broker's orders; while the broker's orders cannot be read, the mandate covers nothing. An order it sent that went unanswered, or that the app's exit cut off, has the host lock orders, since nothing more should go while the account's state is not known.
- `./order` is an order as every broker takes it and an account as every broker reads it. What a position is worth at a price, and what an order would leave the account holding once it fills whole at its limit price, before any fee or tax, are pure functions here, so a window and the agent work them out alike. The tick nearest a price (`nearestTick` in `./risk`) and a Taiwan trade's commission and tax (`twTradeCosts` in `./rules/tw`) are pure functions too, so the order panel prices an order as the checks read it.
- `./sectors` lists what each market's sectors are read from: the Taiwan Stock Exchange's own sector indices in its current classification, leaving out the indices that add sectors up, so no listing counts twice, and for the US, where no source quotes an index of its sectors, State Street's Select Sector funds, one per S&P 500 sector, whose value traded is the fund's own rather than its sector's. Each index's move from its reference and its sector's part of the value all of them traded are a pure function here, so the overview's map and the agent cite the same figures. Each index is drawn from one of the exchange's industry codes, and which of its companies moved it, each one's shares times its change over the exchange's last session, is a pure function here too, so the overview and `get_sector_movers` name the same companies.
- `./rotation` plots a listing or a sector index against a benchmark as a relative rotation graph: the level is the rolling z-score of their price ratio and the momentum the rolling z-score of the level's change, both over one period, so a reading takes twice that many bars and a bar either lacks breaks every window it falls in. The quadrants meet at the origin. It is not the JdK RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum, whose centre is 100 and whose smoothing is unpublished, and reads as a listing's place against its own recent ratio, not as its return against the benchmark, which `relativeReturn` in `./indicators` gives. A reading is of sessions or of closed weeks, each week merged from the market's calendar and dated by its last session, with the week under way left out until its last regular session has closed (`weekUnderWay`), and the benchmark's bars matched by session or by week. Each bar's place is read from that bar and the ones before it alone, so a reading as of an earlier session is cut from the newest path (`readingThrough`), a week dated by its last session and so left out of a reading as of a session inside it; a graph is handed the paths over the sessions it may be taken back to (`RotationHistory`) and cuts each reading itself. Any listing or index may stand as the benchmark, but one other than the market's own index must qualify (`benchmarkFitness`): traded on nearly every one of the market's last `BENCHMARK_SESSIONS` sessions and never missing `BENCHMARK_GAP` in a row, since a bar it lacks breaks every window it falls in. Bars are dated on the market's calendar once (`dateBars`) and the rolling z-score reads every window in one pass, so a market's worth of readings costs milliseconds. The overview's chart and the agent read the same readings over the same periods and trail.
- `./news` reads a listing's stored records with one pure function, `readNews`, which groups them into stories once and derives the gauge and each day's stance from those stories, so the symbol page and the agent count the same stories.
  - Items of one channel whose titles match exactly, such as reprints and a thread's replies, form one story, and so do items of one voice whose titles reword each other within a day, such as several outlets reporting one event.
  - A title is compared without the listing's code and names, its tags and the labels a site sets around its headlines, since those make unrelated titles alike, so the rewording rule waits until the exchange's names for the listing are known.
  - Items of one voice that two sites published on one exchange day are one story too when their vectors read alike, above the line `STORY_LINES` holds for their space. A site's own pages and series, and reports of consecutive days, read alike while telling different stories, and a space with no line joins none.
  - A story's voice and its weight among headlines follow who speaks in it: the decisions model's reading of its lead where it is sure (`SPEAKER_CONFIDENCE`), or else its channel's speaker, since a news search also finds forum posts and data pages. A page of data counts as no news about the listing.
  - Headlines join stories that share an item, as one found for several listings does, and stories whose leads read alike by the same rule and line that join a listing's items.
  - A story's score is a reading of its text and is never redone for its age, only once a source gives its item a new title or snippet; its bearing on now fades instead, halving every `HALF_LIFE_MS` of its voice in the gauge and among headlines alike, the crowd's sooner than the press's. A gauge's reading resting on less faded weight than `RECENT_WEIGHT_LINE` says it is not recent rather than standing for now, and is never shown as neutral. Each day's stance is that day's alone and does not fade.
- `./embedding` is the `Embedder` contract, `cosine` and `clearlyNearest`, which keeps only what reads clearly nearer a query than the rest, since a query nothing answers still has a nearest item. An embedder's `space` names its model and vector length; vectors from different spaces never compare, so a vector travels with its space as an `Embedding`. `storyText` in `./news` says what a news item's vector reads.
- `./web-search` is the web as every vendor must serve it: a search takes structured filters (kind, first day, hosts, market) that each vendor's module translates, and its results carry `Published` times already read on the market's calendar. Searching and reading are separate contracts, so a vendor that only searches can still serve one.
- `./memory` is what the agent keeps across conversations. Its description and body schemas refuse text that looks like a key, token or ID number, and the agent's writes and the user's edits both pass through them, so no memory stores a secret in plain text. `memoryText` says what a memory's vector reads, and `MEMORY_LINES`, by space, how alike two memories read when one only says the other again.
- `./fundamentals` is a listing's filed figures as every provider must serve them, and the limits the exchange puts on its trading, what it is as a fund, the multiples the exchange works out for it, the exchange's notices of its shares going ex and its shareholders' meetings, what the exchanges and the regulator keep on record of how it is run (how much of their shares its directors have pledged, sanctions, the exchange's fines and changes of control), as a source of the exchange's own lists serves them, a whole market's at once and only what is listed today, a fund's kind kept in the exchange's words and a multiple the exchange's own figure, never the app's: a statement holds one quarter alone, in the market's currency, with the day its figures were public, a balance sheet what was held and owed on a quarter's last day, its three borrowing lines as filed rather than added up, since long-term debt due within a year is filed apart from them, a cash flow one quarter alone with capital expenditure as what was paid, and a distribution its cash and stock per share with the day it was announced, `null` where no announcement carries it, as for a fund's, and the days its shares go ex and the cash is paid, each `null` until the company sets it. What a listed fund reports of itself, its newest net asset value and what it paid out of its assets over a month, comes from a source of those reports, a whole market's at once too: the value in the market's currency for the session it was struck for, and each cost in percent of the fund's net assets over the month, as the source gives it. A price's premium or discount to a fund's net asset value is a pure function here. Where a listing's multiples stand among those of the other listings its exchanges class in its industry, each multiple among the ones that have it, is a pure function here too. Growth, margins, trailing EPS, the current ratio, liabilities against assets, free cash flow and the four quarters of cash flow each quarter closes, the cash gone ex over a year, and the price-to-earnings multiple are pure functions here, so the agent cites what the app computed, and a past multiple is priced only against the results public on its day.
- `./flows` is who trades a listing and its market, as every provider must serve it, session by session, in shares of a listing or the market's currency for the market. Net buying, its run of sessions on one side and the change of a balance over a session, a week and a month of sessions are pure functions here, so the agent cites what the app computed.
- `./calendar` derives a listing's coming events from its fundamentals as a pure function: each quarter's statements and month's revenue not out yet on the day the market's rules have them due, which is the latest day rather than the day they come, each distribution's ex and payment days as the company set them, the days its exchange gives notice its shares go ex where no distribution tells of that day, which may come before the amount is announced and is all that tells of a cash capital increase, each shareholders' meeting, and again where it elects directors, and each restriction on its first day, or today while it is in force. Only Taiwan's rules set filing deadlines; elsewhere a quarter's results come around each day the listing's source expects them, as the newest quarter to end before that day, counted on from the newest quarter out by month ends, so a release whose statements the source has yet to take in still names its own quarter. `reportedEvents` reads the dates a listing's report holds the same way. `ListingEvents` is the shape a host serves one listing's in, with whether every source of them was read, and `UpcomingEvents` the shape the agent reads them all in at once, the report's dates apart from the listing's own since they are what the agent found rather than a filing, with their markets' closures and releases, each release with what is known of its figures and kept for `RELEASED_DAYS` once its day has come, and whatever could not be read.
- `./exchange-rate` is the US dollar's close in Taiwan dollars on one day, as a source of it must serve it, and what an amount in either currency is in Taiwan dollars at it, a pure function so a window and the agent read the two currencies together alike. It is for reading them together and nothing else: an order, the risk checks and the trading mandate each stay in their own currency, since a close of the day before is not what a broker would change the money at.
- `./macro` is a market's schedule of economic releases as every source must serve it: each figure as a `MacroIndicator`, so sources of several markets name the same figure alike, on its day or the latest day its agency set, at its time on the exchange's clock where the schedule gives one, with the period it covers and the page its agency publishes it on. An indicator's figures are `MacroSeries`, each a `MacroFigure` in a unit that says how it reads, levels or, where a source reports no level, growth on the year, only the periods already out, each with what the market expected of it where the source says, and what it expects of the next period once that is scheduled, which `releaseExpectations` joins to a release. Each period against the one before and its growth on the year, which an index and a sum have and a score or a rate does not, and what is known of a release's figures, its own period once that is out or else the newest out before it, are pure functions here, so the overview and the agent cite what the app computed.
- `./yields` is US Treasury debt's par yield curve at each day's close as a source must serve it, and how far ten years' yield stands above two years', a pure function so the overview and the agent cite one spread.
- `./schedule` is a message the agent is sent on its own, as the user wrote it or let the agent write it: what to send, when, and how a run nobody watches gets past a call that must ask. `ScheduleApproval` names waiting and the conversation's own check and no way to let every call run, so no scheduled run can be set to bypass. A task recurs every so often, at a time of day on its own clock, which may keep to the days a market trades, read on the exchange's calendar whatever clock the task keeps, or once something the app watches has changed within the scope it names, no sooner than its shortest span: the kinds of change it runs for, and the listings and themes they come from, every listing the user holds and watches and every theme unless it names some. A run keeps the changes it was started for. A `CollectionPlan` times what the app collects on its own with the same shapes but for the change, and `collectionDue` counts from whoever collected last and has no grace, since what is collected reaches back days. `isDue` and `nextRun` are pure: a run counts from the later of when the task was saved and when it last ran, and a time of day the app slept through is made up for once within `FIXED_TIME_GRACE_MS`, older ones dropped.
- `./changes` tells what changed after a moment in what the app already keeps, as a pure function, so telling costs no request: a report's falsifier or a theme's signpost that news was read as stating since then, each once, and a report's event whose day went by. It tells only what a task's scope names, which is what a task that waits on a change runs for, and what such a run is told.
- `./theme` is a topic in the world the user has the app watch because it could reach what they hold over quarters though it moves no price now: why it may matter, what a news search asks for, the listings it could reach, and signposts, each one thing that happens. The `ThemeDesk` keeps what a search found and reads each of a theme's newest items against each signpost once, with the auditor and line of claims, so an item found before a decisions model was set up or before a signpost was written is read then. An item read as stating a signpost is a development, a lead rather than a fact, and a theme's quiet counts from its newest development, not from the news, beside the items not yet read against every signpost, of which it says nothing.
- `./research` is what the agent holds of a listing: a report (`./report`), which carries a view over quarters and is revised, every revision kept, and the forecasts (`./forecast`) made under it, each frozen once made and scored against what the price then did. Neither decides or checks an order: the `OrderDesk` only keeps with each proposal the report in force and the newest forecast made under it as they stood (`ProposalBasis`), which its host reads through `ResearchDesk.basis`, so neither the agent nor the user picks what a proposal is reviewed against. Their text passes the same secret check a memory's does.
  - A listing without a report takes no forecast, and a session takes one. The `ResearchDesk` stamps a forecast's anchor, the newest daily bar, and its report revision itself, so a forecast cannot choose what it is measured from. It stamps a report with the newest quarter public when it was revised: once a newer one is out, the listing takes no forecast until the report is revised.
  - A forecast's scenarios are bands that hold every price of the horizon's close exactly once, so one always comes true and its probabilities can be scored. The horizon counts daily bars after the anchor rather than dates, so judging needs no calendar; `judgeForecast` settles every tie that daily bars leave against the plan, and `forecastTimeline` places those same sessions on a chart's time axis.
  - A forecast is kept with the base rates of its own bands: how often the listing's past moves over that horizon, carried from the anchor, land in each. The record measures settled forecasts against them, since a Brier score alone cannot tell a view from a band wide enough to hold most of history, and a forecast whose bars held too few moves takes no part in that.
  - A forecast's direction is the way its probabilities lean from those base rates, what they add to the bands that lie at or above the anchor less what they add to those that lie below it, a band with prices on both sides counting for neither, and the desk refuses a long or a short the numbers lean away from, so a direction never says what the probabilities do not. A plan is apart from it: a long or a short takes one only where it would be traded, a neutral forecast never.
  - A horizon's bars are judged on the anchor's terms, the close and the plan alike, and base rates are measured from moves on the same terms, both from the bars' references, since a price falls by a dividend, a split or stock distributed with nothing lost and rises by a capital reduction with nothing gained. A bar without a reference moved none. An outcome keeps the close as it traded beside the close it was judged at. An outcome is kept once, so the horizon's session is judged only once its bar has its reference or a later session's bar has come, after which its source gives it none.
  - A report's stance is measured too: each stretch it kept one stance, from the day a revision took it to the day another changed it, against its market's index over the same sessions, both on total return: the listing's bars carried over what went ex, split or was reduced from their references, and the index with what its members paid out reinvested, as `MarketReturns` serves it, since a price index falls with its members' dividends while their splits leave it whole. A stretch ends no later than the newest session the index has. `Coverage` carries them, and none while the bars or the index cannot be read.
  - A claim is a fact with its source and the source's own words, and a report's driver or risk adds a point, the agent's own reading of that fact, so judgement and evidence stay apart. Where the user set up a decisions model the desk has each fact read against its quote and refuses one whose quote says less than it asserts, never a point; a claim no model read is kept as unread.
  - What a report in force has yet to take in, the news read as stating one of its falsifiers and its events gone by, is its backlog, which `Coverage` carries and `backlog` reads from the store alone, so a host can list it for every listing the user follows at no request.
  - A report's event is a date ahead with the source's own words for it and how surely it falls on that day, read as a claim (`eventClaim`) and refused like one when its quote does not give the day. A draft names no event behind the exchange's day; one carried over stays once its day has passed, and `Coverage` names it as passed until a revision drops it and says what came of it.
  - A forecast's scenarios may be put to a vote before it is kept (`./council`), each as a motion of its own: that the close is more likely to land in its band than its base rate says, which is a question a vote can answer where a whole set of hedged probabilities leaves nothing to be for or against. The desk asks only once every check has passed and only of a forecast with base rates, and keeps the forecast however the vote went with each scenario's count beside it. The record counts the scenarios a vote carried against how many history alone would have the close land in.
  - Where the user set up a decisions model, the desk has it read how each fact a forecast rests on bears on the share price, each alone and only once every check has passed, and keeps the forecast with their mean, which the vote is shown. It is a reading of words and not a probability of where the close lands, so it decides nothing, and the record only counts the side the facts read towards, above or below the anchor, against what history expects of the bands on that side. Facts that lean less than `EVIDENCE_LEAN_LINE` call no side, and a fact no model read is left out.
  - The reading and the vote each wait on a model, so the desk checks a forecast again just before it keeps it: a report revised meanwhile is the one it is kept under or refused by, and a forecast kept for the session meanwhile refuses it.
  - Given an embedder, the desk also searches by meaning: every passage of every revision and forecast is embedded once by its text, and the ranking is fused with the search by words, which goes on alone while the embedder cannot be reached.
  - The desk reads each of a report's falsifiers against the news whose vectors lie nearest it, above the floor `FALSIFIER_FLOORS` holds for their space, with the same auditor and line as claims, once per falsifier and item. A listing's passes run one at a time, so none reads an item again that the one before it read. It keeps each pass, failed or not, counting each item the model could not read once, and `Coverage` carries the newest over the revision in force with each `WatchGap` that keeps the falsifiers from being read, so a report no news was read against is never taken for a quiet one.
