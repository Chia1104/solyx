# `@solyx/core`

The trading domain every other package builds on: market rules (tick sizes, board and odd lots, trading sessions), candles and technical indicators, the `BrokerAdapter`, `MarketDataProvider`, `MarketDataStream`, `NewsSource`, `FundamentalsProvider`, `ExchangeListsProvider`, `FlowsProvider`, `TradingCalendarProvider`, `ClosuresProvider`, `MacroCalendarProvider`, `MacroFiguresProvider`, `SentimentScorer`, `Embedder`, `WebSearch`, `WebReader`, `MemoryStore` and `ResearchStore` contracts, the `checkOrder` risk checks, the `OrderDesk` order flow and the `ResearchDesk`, which keeps reports and scored forecasts.

## Boundaries

- Pure and cross-runtime: no I/O and no Node or DOM APIs, so every process imports it. Implementations of its contracts live in their own packages.
- A listing is its market and its exchange's code and nothing more. `./rules/tw` tells the tick a Taiwan listing takes from its code, since the exchanges number funds, notes and real estate trusts apart from stocks, so no order says what kind of listing it is for.
- Technical indicators are pure functions, one value per bar and `null` while warming up, so the charts and the agent read the same numbers.
- `./levels` derives the prices a chart marks from its bars, daily and intraday. They are pure functions too, so the chart and the agent cite the same levels.
- `./session` counts regular sessions on the days a market trades, which the caller hands it: the days its exchange set, and outside their span every weekday, the rule for a market whose holidays are not known. `getSession` reads the same days, so a market is closed all day on one it does not trade, and the `OrderDesk` checks an order's session on the days its host hands it; neither knows a closure called on the day, as for a typhoon, or an early close. A `MarketClosure` is a weekday an exchange has set its market closed with what it calls the day, which only names the day: whether a day trades is the trading days' to say.
- `./quote` reduces a listing's intraday bars to its newest session against the last close before it, so the symbols pane and the overview's heat map read the same numbers, and the move from that close is a pure function here too, which the agent's quotes cite.
- `./sectors` lists the Taiwan Stock Exchange's own sector indices in its current classification and leaves out the indices that add sectors up, so no listing counts twice. Each index's move from its reference and its sector's part of the value all of them traded are a pure function here, so the overview's map and the agent cite the same figures.
- `./news` reads a listing's stored records with one pure function, `readNews`, which groups them into stories once and derives the gauge and each day's stance from those stories, so the symbol page and the agent count the same stories.
  - Items of one channel whose titles match exactly, such as reprints and a thread's replies, form one story, and so do items of one voice whose titles reword each other within a day, such as several outlets reporting one event.
  - A title is compared without the listing's code and names, its tags and the labels a site sets around its headlines, since those make unrelated titles alike, so the rewording rule waits until the exchange's names for the listing are known.
  - Items of one voice that two sites published on one exchange day are one story too when their vectors read alike, above the line `STORY_LINES` holds for their space. A site's own pages and series, and reports of consecutive days, read alike while telling different stories, and a space with no line joins none.
  - A story's voice and its weight among headlines follow who speaks in it: the decisions model's reading of its lead where it is sure (`SPEAKER_CONFIDENCE`), or else its channel's speaker, since a news search also finds forum posts and data pages. A page of data counts as no news about the listing.
  - Headlines join stories that share an item, as one found for several listings does, and stories whose leads read alike by the same rule and line that join a listing's items.
- `./embedding` is the `Embedder` contract, `cosine` and `clearlyNearest`, which keeps only what reads clearly nearer a query than the rest, since a query nothing answers still has a nearest item. An embedder's `space` names its model and vector length; vectors from different spaces never compare, so a vector travels with its space as an `Embedding`. `storyText` in `./news` says what a news item's vector reads.
- `./web-search` is the web as every vendor must serve it: a search takes structured filters (kind, first day, hosts, market) that each vendor's module translates, and its results carry `Published` times already read on the market's calendar. Searching and reading are separate contracts, so a vendor that only searches can still serve one.
- `./memory` is what the agent keeps across conversations. Its description and body schemas refuse text that looks like a key, token or ID number, and the agent's writes and the user's edits both pass through them, so no memory stores a secret in plain text. `memoryText` says what a memory's vector reads, and `MEMORY_LINES`, by space, how alike two memories read when one only says the other again.
- `./fundamentals` is a listing's filed figures as every provider must serve them, and the limits the exchange puts on its trading, what it is as a fund, the multiples the exchange works out for it, the exchange's notices of its shares going ex and its shareholders' meetings, as a source of the exchange's own lists serves them, a whole market's at once and only what is listed today, a fund's kind kept in the exchange's words and a multiple the exchange's own figure, never the app's: a statement holds one quarter alone, in the market's currency, with the day its figures were public, a balance sheet what was held and owed on a quarter's last day, its three borrowing lines as filed rather than added up, since long-term debt due within a year is filed apart from them, a cash flow one quarter alone with capital expenditure as what was paid, and a distribution its cash and stock per share with the day it was announced, `null` where no announcement carries it, as for a fund's, and the days its shares go ex and the cash is paid, each `null` until the company sets it. Where a listing's multiples stand among those of the other listings its exchanges class in its industry, each multiple among the ones that have it, is a pure function here too. Growth, margins, trailing EPS, the current ratio, liabilities against assets, free cash flow and the four quarters of cash flow each quarter closes, the cash gone ex over a year and by each of a run of days, and the price-to-earnings multiple are pure functions here, so the agent cites what the app computed, and a past multiple is priced only against the results public on its day.
- `./flows` is who trades a listing and its market, as every provider must serve it, session by session, in shares of a listing or the market's currency for the market. Net buying, its run of sessions on one side and the change of a balance over a session, a week and a month of sessions are pure functions here, so the agent cites what the app computed.
- `./calendar` derives a listing's coming events from its fundamentals as a pure function: each quarter's statements and month's revenue not out yet on the day the market's rules have them due, which is the latest day rather than the day they come, each distribution's ex and payment days as the company set them, the days its exchange gives notice its shares go ex where no distribution tells of that day, which may come before the amount is announced and is all that tells of a cash capital increase, each shareholders' meeting, and again where it elects directors, and each restriction on its first day, or today while it is in force. Only a market whose rules set filing deadlines, Taiwan's, has filings on its calendar. `reportedEvents` reads the dates a listing's report holds the same way. `UpcomingEvents` is the shape a host serves them in, the report's dates apart from the listing's own since they are what the agent found rather than a filing, with their markets' closures and releases, each release with what is known of its figures and kept for `RELEASED_DAYS` once its day has come, and whatever could not be read, so the overview and the agent read one contract.
- `./macro` is a market's schedule of economic releases as every source must serve it: each figure as a `MacroIndicator`, so sources of several markets name the same figure alike, on its day or the latest day its agency set, at its time on the exchange's clock where the schedule gives one, with the period it covers and the page its agency publishes it on. An indicator's figures are `MacroSeries`, each a `MacroFigure` in a unit that says how it reads, levels alone and only the periods already out. Each period against the one before and its growth on the year, which an index and a sum have and a score or a rate does not, and what is known of a release's figures, its own period once that is out or else the newest out before it, are pure functions here, so the overview and the agent cite what the app computed.
- `./schedule` is a message the agent is sent on its own, as the user wrote it or let the agent write it: what to send, when, and how a run nobody watches gets past a call that must ask. `ScheduleApproval` names waiting and the conversation's own check and no way to let every call run, so no scheduled run can be set to bypass. A task recurs every so often, at a time of day on its own clock, which may keep to the days a market trades, read on the exchange's calendar whatever clock the task keeps, or once something the app watches has changed, no sooner than its shortest span. A `CollectionPlan` times what the app collects on its own with the same shapes but for the change, and `collectionDue` counts from whoever collected last and has no grace, since what is collected reaches back days. `isDue` and `nextRun` are pure: a run counts from the later of when the task was saved and when it last ran, and a time of day the app slept through is made up for once within `FIXED_TIME_GRACE_MS`, older ones dropped.
- `./changes` tells what changed after a moment in what the app already keeps, as a pure function, so telling costs no request: a report's falsifier or a theme's signpost that news was read as stating since then, each once, and a report's event whose day went by. It is what a task that waits on a change runs for, and what such a run is told.
- `./theme` is a topic in the world the user has the app watch because it could reach what they hold over quarters though it moves no price now: why it may matter, what a news search asks for, the listings it could reach, and signposts, each one thing that happens. The `ThemeDesk` keeps what a search found and reads each of a theme's newest items against each signpost once, with the auditor and line of claims, so an item found before a decisions model was set up or before a signpost was written is read then. An item read as stating a signpost is a development, a lead rather than a fact, and a theme's quiet counts from its newest development, not from the news, beside the items not yet read against every signpost, of which it says nothing.
- `./research` is what the agent holds of a listing: a report (`./report`), which carries a view over quarters and is revised, every revision kept, and the forecasts (`./forecast`) made under it, each frozen once made and scored against what the price then did. Neither reaches the `OrderDesk`, and their text passes the same secret check a memory's does.
  - A listing without a report takes no forecast, and a session takes one. The `ResearchDesk` stamps a forecast's anchor, the newest daily bar, and its report revision itself, so a forecast cannot choose what it is measured from. It stamps a report with the newest quarter public when it was revised: once a newer one is out, the listing takes no forecast until the report is revised.
  - A forecast's scenarios are bands that hold every price of the horizon's close exactly once, so one always comes true and its probabilities can be scored. The horizon counts daily bars after the anchor rather than dates, so judging needs no calendar; `judgeForecast` settles every tie that daily bars leave against the plan, and `forecastTimeline` places those same sessions on a chart's time axis.
  - A forecast is kept with the base rates of its own bands: how often the listing's past moves over that horizon, carried from the anchor, land in each. The record measures settled forecasts against them, since a Brier score alone cannot tell a view from a band wide enough to hold most of history, and a forecast whose bars held too few moves takes no part in that.
  - A forecast's direction is the way its probabilities lean from those base rates, what they add to the bands that lie at or above the anchor less what they add to those that lie below it, a band with prices on both sides counting for neither, and the desk refuses a long or a short the numbers lean away from, so a direction never says what the probabilities do not. A plan is apart from it: a long or a short takes one only where it would be traded, a neutral forecast never.
  - Cash that goes ex inside a horizon is added back to its bars before the close and the plan are judged, and to the moves base rates are measured from, since a price falls by a dividend with nothing lost. An outcome keeps the close as it traded beside what was added back. The desk settles nothing while the listing's distributions cannot be read, since an outcome is kept once. Stock distributed and splits are not made up for.
  - A report's stance is measured too: each stretch it kept one stance, from the day a revision took it to the day another changed it, against its market's index over the same sessions, on closes as they traded, since the index is a price index that falls with its members' dividends as they do. `Coverage` carries them, and none while the bars cannot be read.
  - A claim is a fact with its source and the source's own words, and a report's driver or risk adds a point, the agent's own reading of that fact, so judgement and evidence stay apart. Where the user set up a decisions model the desk has each fact read against its quote and refuses one whose quote says less than it asserts, never a point; a claim no model read is kept as unread.
  - A report's event is a date ahead with the source's own words for it and how surely it falls on that day, read as a claim (`eventClaim`) and refused like one when its quote does not give the day. A draft names no event behind the exchange's day; one carried over stays once its day has passed, and `Coverage` names it as passed until a revision drops it and says what came of it.
  - A forecast's scenarios may be put to a vote before it is kept (`./council`), each as a motion of its own: that the close is more likely to land in its band than its base rate says, which is a question a vote can answer where a whole set of hedged probabilities leaves nothing to be for or against. The desk asks only once every check has passed and only of a forecast with base rates, and keeps the forecast however the vote went with each scenario's count beside it. The record counts the scenarios a vote carried against how many history alone would have the close land in.
  - Where the user set up a decisions model, the desk has it read how each fact a forecast rests on bears on the share price, each alone and only once every check has passed, and keeps the forecast with their mean, which the vote is shown. It is a reading of words and not a probability of where the close lands, so it decides nothing, and the record only counts the side the facts read towards, above or below the anchor, against what history expects of the bands on that side. Facts that lean less than `EVIDENCE_LEAN_LINE` call no side, and a fact no model read is left out.
  - The reading and the vote each wait on a model, so the desk checks a forecast again just before it keeps it: a report revised meanwhile is the one it is kept under or refused by, and a forecast kept for the session meanwhile refuses it.
  - Given an embedder, the desk also searches by meaning: every passage of every revision and forecast is embedded once by its text, and the ranking is fused with the search by words, which goes on alone while the embedder cannot be reached.
  - The desk reads each of a report's falsifiers against the news whose vectors lie nearest it, above the floor `FALSIFIER_FLOORS` holds for their space, with the same auditor and line as claims, once per falsifier and item. It keeps each pass, failed or not, and `Coverage` carries the newest over the revision in force with each `WatchGap` that keeps the falsifiers from being read, so a report no news was read against is never taken for a quiet one.
