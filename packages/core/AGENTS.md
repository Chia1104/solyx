# `@solyx/core`

The trading domain every other package builds on: market rules (tick sizes, board and odd lots, trading sessions), candles and technical indicators, the `BrokerAdapter`, `MarketDataProvider`, `MarketDataStream`, `NewsSource`, `FundamentalsProvider`, `FlowsProvider`, `TradingCalendarProvider`, `MacroCalendarProvider`, `SentimentScorer`, `Embedder`, `WebSearch`, `WebReader`, `MemoryStore` and `ResearchStore` contracts, the `checkOrder` risk checks, the `OrderDesk` order flow and the `ResearchDesk`, which keeps reports and scored forecasts.

## Boundaries

- Pure and cross-runtime: no I/O and no Node or DOM APIs, so every process imports it. Implementations of its contracts live in their own packages.
- Technical indicators are pure functions, one value per bar and `null` while warming up, so the charts and the agent read the same numbers.
- `./levels` derives the prices a daily chart marks from its bars: the year's range, the volume profile, support zones around the quarter and half-year lines and under the profile's densest trading, and closes breaking below a line. They are pure functions too, so the chart and the agent cite the same levels.
- `./session` counts regular sessions on the days a market trades, which the caller hands it: the days its exchange set, and outside their span every weekday, the rule for a market whose holidays are not known. `getSession` knows no holidays.
- `./quote` reduces a listing's intraday bars to its newest session against the last close before it, so the symbols pane and the overview's heat map read the same numbers.
- `./sectors` lists the Taiwan Stock Exchange's own sector indices in its current classification and leaves out the indices that add sectors up, so no listing counts twice.
- `./news` reads a listing's stored records as a pure function, `readNews`, which groups them into stories once and derives the gauge and each day's stance from those stories, so every view of a listing, the symbol page and the agent alike, counts the same stories. Items of one channel whose titles match exactly, such as reprints and a thread's replies, form one story, and so do items of one voice whose titles reword each other within a day, such as several outlets reporting one event. A title is compared without the listing's code and names, its tags and the labels a site sets around its headlines, since those make unrelated titles alike, so the rewording rule waits until the exchange's names for the listing are known. Items of one voice that two sites published on one exchange day are one story too when their vectors read alike, above the line `STORY_LINES` holds for their space; a site's own pages and series, and reports of consecutive days, read alike while telling different stories, and a space with no line joins none. Sentiment is given overall and for the press and the crowd. A story's voice and its weight among headlines follow who speaks in it: the decisions model's reading of its lead where it is sure (`SPEAKER_CONFIDENCE`), or else its channel's speaker, since a news search also finds forum posts and data pages; a page of data counts as no news about the listing. Headlines rank the stories about several listings by their channel, their reading, how often they were told and how recent they are; stories that share an item, as one found for several listings does, make one headline, and so do stories whose leads read alike by the same rule and line that join a listing's items.
- `./embedding` is the `Embedder` contract, `cosine` and `clearlyNearest`, which keeps only what reads clearly nearer a query than the rest, since a query nothing answers still has a nearest item. An embedder's `space` names its model and vector length; vectors from different spaces never compare, so a vector travels with its space as an `Embedding`. `storyText` in `./news` says what a news item's vector reads.
- `./web-search` is the web as every vendor must serve it: a search takes structured filters (kind, first day, hosts, market) that each vendor's module translates, and its results carry `Published` times already read on the market's calendar. Searching and reading are separate contracts, so a vendor that only searches can still serve one.
- `./memory` is what the agent keeps across conversations. Its description and body schemas refuse text that looks like a key, token or ID number, and the agent's writes and the user's edits both pass through them, so no memory stores a secret in plain text. `memoryText` says what a memory's vector reads, and `MEMORY_LINES`, by space, how alike two memories read when one only says the other again.
- `./fundamentals` is a listing's filed figures, and the limits the exchange puts on its trading, as every provider must serve them: a statement holds one quarter alone, in the market's currency, with the day its figures were public, and a distribution its cash and stock per share with the days its shares go ex and the cash is paid, each `null` until the company sets it. Growth, margins, trailing EPS, the cash gone ex over a year and the price-to-earnings multiple are pure functions here, so the agent cites what the app computed, and a past multiple is priced only against the results public on its day.
- `./flows` is who trades a listing and its market, as every provider must serve it, session by session: each investor group's buying and selling, in shares of a listing or the market's currency for the market, the margin and short balances, foreign investors' holding, and the positions groups hold in the market's index future. Net buying, its run of sessions on one side and the change of a balance over a session, a week and a month of sessions are pure functions here, so the agent cites what the app computed.
- `./calendar` derives a listing's coming events from its fundamentals as a pure function: each quarter's statements and month's revenue not out yet on the day the market's rules have them due, which is the latest day rather than the day they come, each distribution's ex and payment days as the company set them, and each restriction on its first day, or today while it is in force. Only a market whose rules set filing deadlines, Taiwan's, has filings on its calendar. `UpcomingEvents` is the shape a host serves them in, with their markets' releases and whatever could not be read, so the overview and the agent read one contract.
- `./macro` is a market's schedule of economic releases as every source must serve it: each figure as a `MacroIndicator`, so sources of several markets name the same figure alike, on its day or the latest day its agency set, with the period it covers.
- `./research` is what the agent holds of a listing: a report (`./report`), which carries a view over quarters and is revised, every revision kept, and the forecasts (`./forecast`) made under it, each frozen once made and scored against what the price then did. A listing without a report takes no forecast, and a session takes one. The `ResearchDesk` stamps a forecast's anchor, the newest daily bar, and its report revision itself, so a forecast cannot choose what it is measured from, and stamps a report with the newest quarter public when it was revised: once a newer one is out, the listing takes no forecast until the report is revised. A forecast's scenarios are bands that hold every price of the horizon's close exactly once, so one always comes true and its probabilities can be scored; the horizon counts daily bars after the anchor rather than dates, so judging needs no calendar, and `judgeForecast` settles every tie that daily bars leave against the plan. `forecastTimeline` places those same sessions on a chart's time axis, so a path is drawn where its forecast is judged. A claim in either is a fact with its source and the source's own words, and a report's driver or risk adds a point, the agent's own reading of that fact, so judgement and evidence stay apart. Where the user set up a decisions model the desk has each fact read against its quote and refuses one whose quote says less than it asserts, never a point; a claim no model read is kept as unread. A forecast may be put to a vote before it is kept (`./council`): the desk asks only once every check has passed, keeps the forecast with the vote that carried it, and checks again after the vote, since another forecast may have been kept meanwhile. Given an embedder, the desk also searches by meaning: every passage of every revision and forecast (`reportPassages`, `forecastPassages`) is embedded once by its text, a revision or forecast reads as near as its nearest passage, and the ranking is fused with the search by words; while the embedder cannot be reached, search goes on by words. The desk watches a report's falsifiers: it reads each against the news whose vectors lie nearest it, above the floor `FALSIFIER_FLOORS` holds for their space, with the same auditor and line as claims, once per falsifier and item, and coverage lists the items read as stating one. Neither a report nor a forecast reaches the `OrderDesk`. Their text passes the same secret check a memory's does.
