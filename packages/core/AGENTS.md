# `@solyx/core`

The trading domain every other package builds on: market rules (tick sizes, board and odd lots, trading sessions), candles and technical indicators, the contracts the provider packages implement, the `checkOrder` risk checks, the `OrderDesk` order flow and the `ResearchDesk`, which keeps reports and scored forecasts.

## Boundaries

- Pure and cross-runtime: no I/O and no Node or DOM APIs, so every process imports it. Implementations of its contracts live in their own packages.
- Derived figures (indicators, levels, quotes, fundamentals, flows, sectors, rotation, macro, yields, positions, costs) are pure functions here so every window and the agent cite the same numbers. Indicators give one value per bar and `null` while warming up.
- A listing is its market and its exchange's code and nothing more. `./rules/tw` tells the tick a Taiwan listing takes from its code, since the exchanges number funds, notes and real estate trusts apart from stocks, so no order says what kind of listing it is for.
- `./session` counts regular sessions on the trading days the caller hands it: the days its exchange set, early closes among them, and outside their span every weekday in full, for a market whose holidays are not known. A US early close ends its regular session at 13:00 and its after-hours at 17:00 New York time. `getSession` reads the same days, so a market is closed all day on one it does not trade, and the `OrderDesk` checks an order's session on the days its host hands it; neither knows a closure called on the day, as for a typhoon. A `MarketClosure` only names a weekday an exchange closed; whether a day trades is the trading days' to say.
- `./quote` reduces a listing's intraday bars to its newest session against the last close before it.
- `./risk` holds the checks every order passes before it is proposed and again before it is sent.
  - While the user has locked orders, every order is refused. Once a currency's sales have lost the user's limit over a market's day, or while the broker has yet to reckon one of them or cannot say what the account can spend in it, buys in that currency are refused while sales, which only take risk off, still go.
  - A sale draws only on held shares that working sales do not hold back already, as the broker reports them. The day's loss is read from the broker's fills, each sale's gain over the average cost of what it sold, and only for a buy that has a limit.
  - The desk confirms one proposal at a time, each checked once the one before has the broker's answer, so the cash, shares and fills a check reads are what the orders before it left.
  - A proposal's premise is the session the order went to, the listing's last price and the band then. `premiseViolations` does not compare a price that cannot be read, and refuses a proposal that kept no premise.
  - A listing's price limits are its exchange's, set after a session for the next one, and `sessionBand` holds them for that session alone, so between its close and the next limits coming out the band is unknown. A market order is sized at its band's high, the most it could fill at, so one is refused where the listing trades without limits or its band is unknown; a limit order with no band known goes unchecked against one.
- `./backtest` runs a long-only strategy of declared rules over a listing's bars.
  - Each rule is read at a bar's close from that bar and the ones before it and acted on at the next bar's open, so no rule can see a later price. Rules are data rather than code, so an agent's script asks for a backtest and the engine alone decides every fill; a strategy refuses a key it does not know, so a misspelt stop fails rather than going unread.
  - Costs are its market's (a Taiwan broker's commission and tax, or the SEC's fee on US sales and the tax the US withholds from a Taiwan resident's dividends) plus a tick of slippage, and a bar locked at its limit fills nothing.
  - Indicators read prices adjusted from the bars' references, so a split or what went ex reads as no move, each bar carried to its own terms so a later one changes none; a rule against a price reads it as traded. Orders fill at prices as traded, and a holding carries over a split or what went ex at the reference less the tax its costs withhold, taken from no more cash than the reference made up for. Stops and buy-and-hold carry over the same way, so holding pays that tax too, and `backtestBars` reads distributions only for a run that withholds it.
  - A record is measured before and after a split day apart, each stretch beside holding the listing through it with its deepest fall, its Sharpe ratio of the account's session returns, and a p-value of its timing against `BACKTEST_PERMUTATIONS` orderings of its bars. A trade is timed by its bars and equity is the account at each session's close, so a result grows with the sessions rather than the bars; a result, a walk forward's too, keeps the account's value and holding's at each session's close.
  - A rule may read a line a strategy's own code worked out (`line`), one value a bar, computed on `strategyBars`, the run's bars adjusted on the first bar's terms so no later bar changes an earlier one. A rule compares such a line only with another of the strategy's lines or a value, since bars and indicators are on other terms. Lines count apart from the engine's series, at most `MAX_LINE_VALUES` a run, and each strategy of a scan reads its own.
  - Rule lines, fill prices, limit locks and sessions are worked out here as columns; the `BacktestKernel` the caller hands it runs the bars and holds no rule of a market.
  - On bars shorter than a day, a session's first bar fills after the gap from the close before, the sessions' references come from the daily bars the caller hands it, applied where one went ex or split, and a bar is locked when it traded at one price, its session's limit, which `twPriceLimits` works out from the reference.
  - `scanBacktests` runs strategies each on its own over one set of lines and picks a grid's plateau, the cell whose neighbourhood had the highest mean Sharpe ratio, in-sample where there is a split. `walkForward` picks on each window's training the same way and runs the picks on the sessions after as one test with the holding carried, so its record is of picking on the past alone.
  - `rollingBacktest` runs one strategy over whole windows of the same calendar months, one from the first session of every step, each from holding nothing at the close before it, so how much a record owes to its start shows. It sums them beside holding and counts how many fit end to end, since overlapping windows share bars, and tests no window's timing.
  - `backtestBars` reads a listing's bars, with the daily bars that shorter ones take references from, so the agent's backtests and a window's read alike.
- `./strategy` is a strategy the user writes: a folder whose `STRATEGY.md` frontmatter names the command its code runs by, its numbers (bounded and stepped as a template's are) and the rules the app trades on, which read the lines the code works out. The code reads `strategyInput` and writes each job's lines, a NaN or an infinity read as no value; it never fills, prices or costs a trade. `strategyJobs` runs the first job again over the first fifths of the bars, and `lookAhead` names the first bar where a line differs from the whole run's; that is a warning, not a refusal, since the user owns the code. `strategyPathSchema` keeps a changed file's path inside the strategy's folder and out of what tools keep beside the code (`STRATEGY_LEFT_OUT`), which is no part of a version.
- `./strategy-templates` sets a backtest up without writing rules: each template is a rule traders name by a few numbers, each with bounds and steps, and the years of daily bars that hold about twenty of its trades each side of a split. A setup refuses a number its template does not take. `gridProblem` says as data why a grid would not run, counting each axis before making its values, so a window can say so before it asks.
- `./review` replays the broker's fills into round trips, a position from the buy that opened it to the sale that left none, each under the stance of the report its first buy's proposal kept. What the account held before the fills begin is what it holds now less what they bought and sold, and a position held then opens no trip until it is sold off, since its first buy is not among them.
- `./broker` is a broker as the `OrderDesk` uses it.
  - The order tag (`orderTag`) is six letters and digits, which every broker's own field takes. `reconcile` only reads; the desk tries it after each unanswered sending, and its host as it opens, when the user asks and on `brokerChanged`.
  - A proposal keeps the broker it was made for by `brokerId`, which names the account where the broker has several; a desk opened on another dismisses it while it waits and never looks for its order.
  - What a Taiwan account can spend (`twSpendable` in `./rules/tw`) is its settlement bank's balance with every unsettled trade against it, since trades settle on the second trading day after, today's at the most commission and tax so it errs low.
- An exit (`Exit` in `./order`) fires while the app is closed: once the listing trades at or below its stop it sells at the day's lower limit, and at or above its target at that price. `checkExit` refuses one whose price the listing has reached already, or that sells shares held back by working sales and other exits, and its last day falls from tomorrow to `MAX_EXIT_DAYS` on. Confirming places each price as a leg, all or none. A leg carries no tag, so the desk finds an unanswered exit's legs among those the broker watches by what they sell and when. `reconcileExits` cancels the rest once a leg is no longer watched (sold, expired or taken off); while the app is closed a second leg may sell only shares the account still holds.
- `./mandate` covers the agent's limit orders on the listings and sides it names, each order and each market day under values set per currency and a count of orders, until it expires. `mandateViolations` says why it does not cover an order, as data.
  - The desk puts each agent proposal that passed every check to it as it is made and keeps the verdict. One it covers outside shadow goes the way a confirmation does, checked again with the mandate read afresh, so a mandate changed or revoked meanwhile leaves it waiting for the user.
  - A day's count is of what the mandate covered, in shadow too, and of every order the broker lists for that day under the desk's tag, the user's own confirmed orders among them; a proposal counts only where the broker lists no order of it, since a restored backup or an edit of the store changes proposals but not the broker's orders. While the broker's orders cannot be read, the mandate covers nothing.
  - An order it sent that went unanswered, or that the app's exit cut off, has the host lock orders, since nothing more should go while the account's state is not known.
- `./order` holds an order as every broker takes it and an account as every broker reads it. What an order would leave the account holding is worked out filled whole at its limit price, before any fee or tax. `nearestTick` in `./risk` and `twTradeCosts` in `./rules/tw` let a window price an order as the checks read it.
- `./sectors`: Taiwan's sectors are the Taiwan Stock Exchange's own sector indices in its current classification, leaving out the indices that add sectors up so no listing counts twice. The US's are State Street's Select Sector funds, one per S&P 500 sector, since no source quotes a sector index; their value traded is the fund's own rather than its sector's. A sector's movers are its industry code's companies, each weighed by shares times change over the exchange's last session.
- `./rotation` is a relative rotation graph: the level is the rolling z-score of the price ratio to a benchmark and the momentum the rolling z-score of the level's change, both over one period, so a reading takes twice that many bars and a bar either side lacks breaks every window it falls in. The quadrants meet at the origin. It is not the JdK RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum (centred on 100, unpublished smoothing) and reads a listing's place against its own recent ratio, not its return against the benchmark, which `relativeReturn` in `./indicators` gives.
  - A reading is of sessions or of closed weeks, each week dated by its last session and left out until that session has closed (`weekUnderWay`). Each bar's place is read from it and the ones before it alone, so a reading as of an earlier session is cut from the newest path (`readingThrough`); a graph is handed the paths over the sessions it may go back to (`RotationHistory`) and cuts each reading itself.
  - A benchmark other than the market's own index must qualify (`benchmarkFitness`): traded on nearly every one of the market's last `BENCHMARK_SESSIONS` sessions and never missing `BENCHMARK_GAP` in a row.
  - Bars are dated on the market's calendar once (`dateBars`) and the rolling z-score reads every window in one pass, so a market's readings cost milliseconds.
- `./news` reads a listing's stored records with one pure function, `readNews`, which groups them into stories once and derives the gauge and each day's stance from those stories.
  - Items of one channel whose titles match exactly, such as reprints and a thread's replies, form one story, and so do items of one voice whose titles reword each other within a day.
  - A title is compared without the listing's code and names, its tags and a site's labels around its headlines, since those make unrelated titles alike, so the rewording rule waits until the exchange's names for the listing are known.
  - Items of one voice that two sites published on one exchange day are one story too when their vectors read alike above the space's `STORY_LINES` line. Items of one site, and reports of consecutive days, read alike while telling different stories; a space with no line joins none.
  - A story's voice and weight follow who speaks in it: the decisions model's reading of its lead where it is sure (`SPEAKER_CONFIDENCE`), or else its channel's speaker, since a news search also finds forum posts and data pages. A page of data counts as no news about the listing.
  - Headlines join stories that share an item, and stories whose leads read alike by the same rule and line.
  - A story's score is a reading of its text, redone only when a source gives its item a new title or snippet, never for its age. Its bearing on now fades instead, halving every `HALF_LIFE_MS` of its voice, the crowd's sooner than the press's. A gauge resting on less faded weight than `RECENT_WEIGHT_LINE` says it is not recent and is never shown as neutral. Each day's stance is that day's alone and does not fade.
- `./embedding`: vectors from different spaces (model and vector length) never compare, so a vector travels with its space as an `Embedding`. `clearlyNearest` keeps only what reads clearly nearer a query than the rest, since a query nothing answers still has a nearest item.
- `./web-search` is the web as every vendor serves it: a search takes structured filters (kind, first day, hosts, market) each vendor's module translates, and results carry `Published` times read on the market's calendar. Searching and reading are separate contracts, so a vendor that only searches can still serve one.
- `./memory` is what the agent keeps across conversations. Its description and body schemas refuse text that looks like a key, token or ID number, and the agent's writes and the user's edits both pass through them, so no memory stores a secret in plain text. `MEMORY_LINES`, by space, says how alike two memories read when one only repeats the other.
- `./fundamentals` is what providers serve of a listing's filed figures and of the exchanges' own lists, the latter a whole market's at once and only what is listed today.
  - A fund's kind stays in the exchange's words and a multiple is the exchange's own figure, never the app's.
  - A statement holds one quarter alone, in the market's currency, with the day its figures were public. A balance sheet keeps its three borrowing lines as filed rather than added up, since long-term debt due within a year is filed apart from them. A cash flow holds one quarter alone, with capital expenditure as what was paid.
  - A distribution's announcement day, ex day and payment day are each `null` until set; a fund's carries no announcement.
  - A fund's reports come a whole market at once: its newest net asset value for the session it was struck for, and each monthly cost in percent of net assets as the source gives it.
  - A past multiple is priced only against the results public on its day.
- `./flows` is who trades a listing and its market, session by session, in shares for a listing or the market's currency for the market.
- `./calendar` derives a listing's coming events from its fundamentals. A filing is placed on the day the market's rules have it due, the latest day rather than the day it comes. An exchange's ex notice may come before a distribution's amount is announced and is all that tells of a cash capital increase. Only Taiwan's rules set filing deadlines; elsewhere a quarter's results come on each day the source expects them, as the newest quarter to end before that day, counted from the newest quarter out by month ends, so a release the source has yet to take in still names its own quarter. `UpcomingEvents` keeps a report's dates apart from the listing's own, since the agent found them rather than a filing, and keeps a release for `RELEASED_DAYS` once its day has come.
- `./exchange-rate` is the US dollar's close in Taiwan dollars on one day, for reading the two currencies together and nothing else: an order, the risk checks and the trading mandate each stay in their own currency, since a close of the day before is not what a broker would change the money at.
- `./macro` is a market's schedule of economic releases. Each figure is a `MacroIndicator`, so sources of several markets name it alike, on its day or the latest day its agency set, at its time on the exchange's clock where given. A `MacroSeries` holds only periods already out, as levels or, where a source reports no level, growth on the year, each with the market's expectation where the source gives it, and the expectation of the next period once it is scheduled. Period-over-period and year-on-year change apply to an index and a sum, not to a score or a rate.
- `./yields` is US Treasury debt's par yield curve at each day's close and the ten-year less two-year spread.
- `./schedule` is a message the agent is sent on its own. `ScheduleApproval` names waiting and the conversation's own check and no way to let every call run, so no scheduled run can be set to bypass.
  - A task recurs every so often, at a time of day on its own clock (optionally on trading days read on the exchange's calendar), or once something the app watches has changed within its scope, no sooner than its shortest span. A scope that names no listings or themes covers every listing the user holds and watches and every theme. A run keeps the changes it was started for.
  - `isDue` and `nextRun` count from the later of when the task was saved and when it last ran, and a time of day the app slept through is made up once within `FIXED_TIME_GRACE_MS`, older ones dropped. `collectionDue` counts a `CollectionPlan` from whoever collected last and has no grace, since what is collected reaches back days.
- `./changes` tells, from what the app already keeps and so at no request, what changed after a moment: a report's falsifier or a theme's signpost that news was read as stating, each once, and a report's event whose day went by. It tells only what a task's scope names.
- `./theme` is a topic the user has the app watch because it could reach what they hold over quarters though it moves no price now. The `ThemeDesk` reads each of a theme's newest items against each signpost once, with the auditor and line of claims, so an item found before a decisions model or a signpost existed is read then. An item read as stating a signpost is a development, a lead rather than a fact. A theme's quiet counts from its newest development, not from the news, and says nothing of the items not yet read against every signpost.
- `./research` is what the agent holds of a listing: a report (`./report`), a view over quarters whose every revision is kept, and the forecasts (`./forecast`) under it, each frozen once made and scored against what the price then did. Neither decides or checks an order: the `OrderDesk` only keeps with each proposal the report in force and the newest forecast under it as they stood (`ProposalBasis`), read through `ResearchDesk.basis`, so neither the agent nor the user picks what a proposal is reviewed against. Their text passes the same secret check a memory's does.
  - A listing without a report takes no forecast, and a session takes one. The `ResearchDesk` stamps a forecast's anchor (the newest daily bar) and report revision itself, so a forecast cannot choose what it is measured from. It stamps a report with the newest quarter public when it was revised; once a newer one is out, the listing takes no forecast until the report is revised.
  - A forecast's scenarios are bands that hold every price of the horizon's close exactly once, so one always comes true and its probabilities can be scored. The horizon counts daily bars after the anchor rather than dates, so judging needs no calendar; `judgeForecast` settles every tie daily bars leave against the plan.
  - A forecast keeps the base rates of its own bands: how often the listing's past moves over that horizon, carried from the anchor, land in each. The record measures settled forecasts against them, since a Brier score alone cannot tell a view from a band wide enough to hold most of history; a forecast whose bars held too few moves takes no part.
  - A forecast's direction is the way its probabilities lean from the base rates, what they add to bands at or above the anchor less what they add below it, a band straddling the anchor counting for neither. The desk refuses a long or a short the numbers lean away from. Only a long or a short takes a plan, and only where it would be traded; a neutral forecast never does.
  - A horizon's bars, the close and the plan alike, are judged on the anchor's terms, and base rates are measured on the same terms, both from the bars' references, since a price falls by a dividend, a split or stock distributed with nothing lost and rises by a capital reduction with nothing gained. A bar without a reference moved none. An outcome keeps the close as traded beside the close it was judged at, and is kept once, so the horizon's session is judged only once its bar has its reference or a later session's bar has come, after which its source gives it none.
  - A report's stance is measured over each stretch it kept one stance against its market's index over the same sessions, both on total return: the listing's bars carried over what went ex, split or was reduced, and the index with its members' payouts reinvested (`MarketReturns`), since a price index falls with its members' dividends while their splits leave it whole. A stretch ends no later than the index's newest session. `Coverage` carries none while the bars or the index cannot be read.
  - A claim is a fact with its source and the source's own words; a report's driver or risk adds a point, the agent's own reading, so judgement and evidence stay apart. Where the user set up a decisions model the desk has each fact read against its quote and refuses one whose quote says less than it asserts, never a point; a claim no model read is kept as unread.
  - A report's backlog, the news read as stating one of its falsifiers and its events gone by, is read from the store alone (`backlog`), so a host can list it for every followed listing at no request.
  - A report's event is a date ahead with the source's own words and how surely it falls on that day, read as a claim (`eventClaim`) and refused when its quote does not give the day. A draft names no event behind the exchange's day; one carried over stays once its day has passed, and `Coverage` names it as passed until a revision drops it and says what came of it.
  - A forecast's scenarios may be put to a vote (`./council`), each as its own motion: that the close is more likely to land in its band than its base rate says, a question a vote can answer where a set of hedged probabilities gives nothing to be for or against. The desk asks only once every check has passed and only of a forecast with base rates, and keeps the forecast however the vote went. The record counts the scenarios a vote carried against how many history alone would have the close land in.
  - Where the user set up a decisions model, the desk has it read how each fact a forecast rests on bears on the share price, each alone and only once every check has passed, and keeps their mean. It is a reading of words, not a probability, so it decides nothing; the record only counts the side the facts read towards against what history expects of the bands on that side. Facts leaning less than `EVIDENCE_LEAN_LINE` call no side, and a fact no model read is left out.
  - The reading and the vote each wait on a model, so the desk checks a forecast again just before keeping it: a report revised meanwhile is the one it is kept under or refused by, and a forecast kept for the session meanwhile refuses it.
  - Given an embedder, the desk embeds every passage of every revision and forecast once by its text and fuses the ranking with the search by words, which goes on alone while the embedder cannot be reached.
  - The desk reads each of a report's falsifiers against the news whose vectors lie nearest it above the space's `FALSIFIER_FLOORS` floor, with the auditor and line of claims, once per falsifier and item. A listing's passes run one at a time, so none rereads an item the one before read. Each pass is kept, failed or not, and `Coverage` carries the newest with each `WatchGap` that keeps the falsifiers from being read, so a report no news was read against is never taken for a quiet one.
